Real-time speech translation at the United Nations General Debate

A comparison of two Exbabel configurations and recorded UN interpreters across six language directions.

Exbabel Flagship scored 4 points higher than the UN interpreter in blind review

On 55 matched excerpts, two AI reviewers independently judged how much of each delegate’s meaning survived. Flagship scored higher on 34 clips and the interpreter on 21. The estimated 95% interval for the mean difference was +0.4 to +6.1 points.

Hear what a score can hide

Spain’s delegate described other challenges as “no menores.” The interpreter called them “just as important.” Flagship said “no less important.” The Translation Model said “minor ones,” reversing the comparison.

Floor identity was checked against the archive designation, automatic language identification and the same-language channel waveform. For China, the catalog row said English, but the selected floor speech and channel match supported Mandarin. One delegation supplied Mandarin and two supplied Russian speech. The cohort comes from one General Debate session; speaker selection and acoustic conditions limit generalization.

A clock-time cut can omit part of a sentence because an interpreter listens before speaking. Even after automatic alignment, a cut can begin after the delegate’s opening phrase. This is visible in the Spanish “no menores” example. A missing phrase in the excerpt does not, by itself, establish an interpreter omission.

COMET uses a learned model to estimate quality from the source, hypothesis and reference. Its score cannot be converted into “percent matched.” BLEU measures corpus-level word n-gram precision with a brevity penalty. chrF++ uses character n-gram agreement plus word n-grams. Single-reference overlap can penalize good paraphrases. See the workbook for BLEU, uncertainty intervals and native-caption results.

SacreBLEU 2.6.0 used 13a tokenization for these English/French targets and word order 2 for chrF++. The written suite also configured zh tokenization for Chinese targets where applicable. COMET used Unbabel/wmt22-comet-da via unbabel-comet 2.2.7 on CPU. Checkpoint SHA-256: e213091cde220f97b89f8bdfa750c458cfea741ad62affb455b59900210ff2af.

Per-direction samples are small. For Mandarin, both engines scored 0.640 on 13 aligned clips. On the further-audited 10-clip subset, Exbabel Flagship Model scored 0.692 and Exbabel Translation Model 0.633. The reference eligibility rule can change an apparent ranking.

Fidelity to the source speaker

Three fresh AI subagents worked independently from masked packets. Two agents rated each candidate. Labels and ordering varied by rater. The 0–100 Direct Assessment rubric gave full credit to meaningful paraphrases and harmless compression. Raters separately flagged consequential changes to actors, quantities, polarity, legal force and key propositions. The professional interpreter received a score under the same rubric.

Exbabel Flagship Model minus Exbabel Translation Model averaged +4.1 DA points (exploratory paired 95% interval +1.8 to +6.5). Exbabel Flagship Model minus interpreter averaged +4.0 (+0.4 to +6.1). Exbabel Translation Model minus interpreter averaged −0.2 (−4.1 to +2.1). Intervals resample delegations within directions, with 10,000 paired draws. Few delegations, automatic selection and related raters limit those intervals.

All six rater/control combinations ranked both faithful paraphrases above the deliberate quantity or polarity error. Ratings were within ten points on 162/165 genuine candidates. The three subagents share an OpenAI model family. High agreement among them cannot substitute for independent bilingual listening.

A stringent sensitivity check discarded an entire three-way case if both raters flagged a possible recognition or boundary artifact on any candidate. It retained zero cases. This does not establish that every translation was wrong. It does mean that the study cannot report a separate artifact-free DA estimate.

The written branch returned 65/65 translations. The recorded-reading branch returned 29/30 Exbabel Flagship Model WAVs and 30/35 Exbabel Translation Model WAVs. The initial text-only Exbabel Translation Model alias returned HTTP 404; the separately logged gemini-3.1-flash-lite run supplied successful text results. Those findings use development material, not the inaccessible official fixed test archive.

Twelve passages from actual prepared statements (two per source language) were separately matched to delivered speeches using floor ASR without looking at either system’s output. Both text models translated each passage, producing 24 outputs. Prepared and spoken wording can differ. The casebook shows all 12 beside the original floor transcript.

Source word error rate uses edit counts divided by reference word count; Chinese uses character units. A known written script supplied the reference for controlled readings. For five Spanish readings the recognizer made 1 edit in 112 reference words. A verified verbatim source transcript is still required to calculate this measure for authentic speech.

Streaming, reliability and the Exbabel worker

Exbabel Flagship Model’s gpt-realtime-translate received paced 24 kHz audio. Exbabel Translation Model’s gemini-3.5-live-translate-preview received paced 16 kHz audio from the same 48 kHz source masters. Logs capture local sends, translated-audio packets, caption events, errors and close signals. Quota retries used new attempt directories. The first captured result, or original failure, supplied the canonical sample.

Exbabel Flagship Model confirmed completion on 180 primary sessions. No Exbabel Translation Model primary session returned the expected final-turn signal before the deadline. The ending classifier nevertheless marked the final recoverable idea present on 142 Exbabel Flagship Model and 118 Exbabel Translation Model assignments; missing or changed on 34 and 51; uncertain on 4 and 7. Four Exbabel Translation Model assignments had no audio. Those are transcript-based flags, pending listening review.

Time to first packet includes silence; it does not measure when the listener understood a translated phrase. The archive retains p50/p95 caption and audio onset, jitter proxies, ASR/caption agreement, active signal levels and exploratory term-anchor timing. A single Bahamas speech supplied clean, 10 dB noise and 80-second continuous probes. Additional probes introduced a two-second mute, four seconds of competing speech, and send delays. These do not determine deployed failover or naturalness.

Seven isolated worker route probes returned speech after recovery, including the explicit Exbabel Translation Model Arabic route. One French clip tested Exbabel Translation Model worker behavior with and without a Ukraine terminology instruction. Both returned audio. This sample cannot estimate glossary benefit. A later expansion of worker testing was stopped at the requester’s clarification. Its partial logs are retained for chronology and excluded from these results.
